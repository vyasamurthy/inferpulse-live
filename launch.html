The Core Metric

Workload
Economic Index

WEI is value divided by cost — computed for every AIM on your AMD cluster every 5 seconds. Below 1.0 you are losing money. Above 1.4 you are in the Scale zone.

Scale (WEI ≥ 1.4) — increase GPU allocation. Highest value-per-dollar in your factory.
Maintain (1.0–1.4) — stable positive economics. Monitor weekly. No action required.
Optimize (0.6–1.0) — declining WEI. Behavioral drift is likely eroding value. Investigate.
Kill (<0.6) — negative economics. Every hour costs more than it produces. Suspend immediately.

Financial Council

Eight algorithms.
One verdict.

Eight independent financial algorithms each compute WEI through a different theoretical lens. A weighted ensemble combines them into a single WEI with a 95% confidence interval.

CAPM — treats AIM WEI like a financial asset. High drift = high beta = higher risk premium required.
DCF Lifecycle — discounts future value delivery using drift as the decay rate. Anchors WEI to the 30-day time-averaged reality, not the spot reading.
Monte Carlo — 10,000 stochastic paths with mean reversion. Returns WEI distribution: P5, P25, P75, P95, and probability of staying above break-even.
Multi-Factor Decomposition — separates WEI into alpha (genuine value above market) vs systematic beta. Alpha is the only number worth paying for.
